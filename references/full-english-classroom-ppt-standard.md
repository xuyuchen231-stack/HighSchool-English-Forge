# Full-English High-Density Classroom PPT Standard

## Language
Every slide must be English-only: titles, body, labels, examples, instructions, exercises, answers, charts and footers. Do not include Chinese headings, translations or bilingual labels. Preserve quoted source English verbatim. Chinese explanations belong in the accompanying study PDF, not the PPT.

## Audience and density
Design for senior-high English classrooms, not a generic AI pitch deck. Each content slide must teach a defined objective and contain substantive material. Prefer 2–4 structured regions: annotated source text, evidence table, vocabulary/collocation table, sentence analysis, foundation/advanced comparison, worked example or practice with answers. Avoid title-only slides, slogan-only slides, oversized decorative cards, empty space and filler pages.

Default 16:9 typography: title 26–32 pt; body 16–21 pt; dense tables 13–16 pt; footnotes 10–12 pt. Keep readable; split genuinely dense content rather than shrinking text. Include examples, not bare lists.

## Visual system
Use a disciplined classroom editorial design: white/off-white background, dark navy headings, restrained blue structure, red for critical evidence/errors and one limited secondary accent. Consistent grid, alignment, margins, line spacing, table rules, highlighting and page numbers. Vary purposeful layouts: annotated passage, two-column comparison, worked example, compact reference table, exercise/answer reveal, plot/logic map. No stock AI imagery, random illustrations, meaningless icons, gradients, 3D effects or decorative blobs.

## Writing deck coverage
For a complete writing course cover both application writing and continuation writing, with genre purpose, audience, register, task analysis and scoring criteria; foundation vs advanced expressions in parallel; explained collocations and examples; sentence transformation/combining; annotated model answers; common errors/corrections; guided and independent practice; answer keys and rubrics.

Continuation writing must include source facts vs permissible development vs prohibited contradiction; paragraph-opening analysis; big idea/character arc; small beats/causal event chain; paragraph beat maps; callbacks to source props/foreshadowing; emotion/action/dialogue/sensory language; theme derived from character action; weak-vs-improved ending; practice and annotated model outline.

## QA
Render every slide to images/PDF and inspect at presentation size. Reject sparse, overcrowded, tiny-font, clipped, broken-table, inconsistent or filler slides. Check each slide's distinct teaching purpose, progression, source fidelity and English correctness. A deck is not accepted merely because it opens or has a certain slide count.
