# Continuation writing: big idea, small beats, and theme

## 1. Big idea before sentence writing
Write one sentence describing the character's transformation: **from [initial state] to [new state], because [source-grounded turning point].** Identify:
- character's initial desire/fear/misunderstanding;
- central obstacle;
- planted objects, actions, relationships or setting details;
- the change that the given paragraph starters imply.

## 2. Small beats for each paragraph
Build 4–6 causal beats per paragraph. A reliable sequence is:
1. respond to the given opening sentence;
2. observe/hear a concrete stimulus;
3. show a physical or emotional reaction;
4. attempt an action or make a decision;
5. encounter a small setback or receive support;
6. show visible progress that prepares the next paragraph.

The second paragraph should complete the change, not repeat the first paragraph. Use actions and dialogue to prove emotional change.

## 3. Theme elevation
Move through three levels:
- **Event:** what concretely happened?
- **Character change:** what did the character learn or become able to do?
- **Universal meaning:** what does this suggest about courage, belonging, empathy, responsibility, patience or cooperation?

Use one or two sentences. The theme must be earned by the story. Avoid unsupported claims, overblown moralizing, or a generic "努力就会成功" ending.

## 4. Case calibration: Bayleigh and Popcorn
- Big idea: fear of being judged -> courage to participate, triggered by watching Popcorn persist with a physical challenge.
- Paragraph 1: Mac asks whether Popcorn can climb stairs; Bayleigh observes; Popcorn attempts, slips, tries again and makes progress; Bayleigh offers encouragement.
- Paragraph 2: the kitten's progress awakens Bayleigh's determination; she takes her adapted bike outside and joins neighborhood children; they make room for her.
- Theme: courage can begin with a small step; differences need not prevent belonging; encouragement can be mutual.
- Continuity guard: do not write a sudden cure for cerebral palsy or claim that effort removes disability.

## 5. Case calibration: Hilda, Katherine and Maya
- Big idea: misunderstanding/blame -> discovery/understanding -> cooperation.
- Paragraph 1: Hilda reveals objects hidden behind the filter box and explains Maya's collecting/exchanging behavior; Katherine's confusion becomes surprise.
- Paragraph 2: Katherine recognizes the mistake, apologizes, Hilda forgives; they adapt training to Maya's behavior and turn discovery into a positive opportunity.
- Theme: observe before judging; understanding individual behavior can turn a problem into cooperation.
- Preserve the supplied paragraph starters and the established animal-training context.

## 6. Quality gate
Reject a continuation plan if it ignores the paragraph starters, changes the source facts, introduces an unprepared major event, has no visible action proving the character change, or adds a theme that is not supported by the story.
